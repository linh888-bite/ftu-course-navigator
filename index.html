Essential Academic Information for International Exchange Students

Key rules to understand before selecting courses and registering your schedule at FTU

1. Course Code vs. Section Code

Course Code (e.g., KET317 or QTRE402) identifies the academic subject. Section Code (e.g., KET317.1) identifies the specific semester class group & timetable slot.

2. Credits & 'E'-Coded English Modules

Standard FTU courses carry 3.0 FTU Credits (approx. 45 classroom contact hours). Course codes with 'E' in the 4th character (e.g., QTRE402, QTRE408) denote English-taught modules.

3. Prerequisites & Learning Agreement

If an FTU course lists a prerequisite code (e.g., KET314 for KET317), exchange students who completed equivalent coursework at their home university may verify waiver eligibility with the International Office.

How to Read FTU Course Codes, Class Sections & Timetable Shifts

New international students frequently confuse permanent Course Codes with semester-specific Class Section Codes. Use the visual structure guide and official 13-term glossary below when registering your courses.

A. Subject Course Code
QTRE402 / KET317

• First 3 letters (QTR, KET): Academic department.
• 4th letter 'E' (QTRE): Taught in English.
• 3 digits (402, 317): Course level & number.

B. Class Section Code
KET317.1

• Appears in the semester registration timetable.
• Identifies a specific class group, lecturer, day, shift, and room opened in a given semester.
• One Course Code can have multiple Section Codes (.1, .2, .3).

C. Daily Class Shifts & Rooms
Shift 2 • B302

• Shift 1: 07:00 – 09:25 | Shift 2: 09:35 – 12:00
• Shift 3: 12:30 – 14:55 | Shift 4: 15:05 – 17:30
• Room B302: Building B, 3rd Floor, Room 302.

Official FTU Academic & Registration Glossary (13 Terms)

Course Code Academic Identifier
Official Course Code

Identifies the academic subject in the FTU curriculum. It stays the same across different classes and semesters.

Example: KET317 (Social Responsibility Accounting), QTRE402 (Organizational Behavior)
⚠️ Common Confusion: Students often confuse the Course Code with the Class Section Code used for registration.
Section Code Registration Identifier
Class Section Code

Identifies a specific class opening in a given semester, including its schedule, lecturer, and room.

Example: KET317.1 (Illustrative format)
⚠️ Common Confusion: Different sections of the same course have the same syllabus content but different class times.
Prerequisite Academic Requirement
Prerequisite Course

A course or foundational knowledge that students should complete before enrolling in a more advanced course.

Example: KET314 before KET317
⚠️ Common Confusion: Exchange students may already satisfy this through an equivalent course at their home university.
Credits Academic Workload
FTU Academic Credits

Indicates the academic weight and study workload of a course at FTU.

Example: 3 Credits (Standard 45 contact hours)
⚠️ Common Confusion: FTU credits may need conversion to ECTS or home-university credits via your Learning Agreement.
CLO Syllabus Term
Course Learning Outcomes

Describes the knowledge, skills, and autonomy students are expected to achieve after completing the course.

Example: CLO1, CLO2, CLO3
⚠️ Common Confusion: Learning outcomes describe expected competencies, not the weekly lecture schedule.
Shift 1 Timetable Slot
Morning Shift 1

First morning class session of the day at FTU.

Example: 07:00 – 09:25 (Illustrative)
⚠️ Common Confusion: Check exact start/end times on the official semester timetable.
Shift 2 Timetable Slot
Morning Shift 2

Second morning class session of the day at FTU.

Example: 09:35 – 12:00 (Illustrative)
⚠️ Common Confusion: Two courses in the same day and shift will create a direct schedule conflict.
Shift 3 Timetable Slot
Afternoon Shift 3

First afternoon class session of the day at FTU.

Example: 12:30 – 14:55 (Illustrative)
⚠️ Common Confusion: Verify afternoon session timing for your specific campus building.
Shift 4 Timetable Slot
Afternoon Shift 4

Second afternoon class session of the day at FTU.

Example: 15:05 – 17:30 (Illustrative)
⚠️ Common Confusion: Ensure no overlap with language or seminar sessions.
Room Code (A / B / D) Campus Location
Building & Classroom Code

The letter indicates the campus building, the first digit indicates the floor, and the last two digits indicate the room number.

Example: B302 = Building B, 3rd Floor, Room 302
⚠️ Common Confusion: Do not confuse Building A, Building B, and Building D on the Hanoi campus (91 Chua Lang).
CLC / High-Quality Program Program Type
High-Quality English-Taught Program

Undergraduate tracks at FTU where specialized courses are delivered in English (often marked with "E" in the course code).

Example: QTRE402, QTRE408, QTRE444
⚠️ Common Confusion: Confirm with the International Office which sections are open to exchange students each semester.
Time_Slot_Tag Schedule Check Key
Day & Shift Combination Tag

Combines the day of the week and the class shift to help students detect timetable clashes immediately.

Example: Tuesday - Shift 2
⚠️ Common Confusion: If two shortlisted class sections share the same Time_Slot_Tag, they cannot be taken together.
Data_Verification Data Transparency
Source Verification Status

Indicates whether information comes directly from an official FTU syllabus page or requires semester timetable confirmation.

Example: Verified / Partially Verified
⚠️ Common Confusion: Always verify final exam dates and room assignments on FTUGate.

About Foreign Trade University (FTU)

Foreign Trade University (FTU) is one of the top universities in Vietnam. Established in 1960, FTU has gained enormous achievement in providing talented and high-quality human resources to the economy and thus made great contribution to the industrialization, modernization, and global economic integration of Vietnam. The annual admissions test to the university is among the most competitive in Vietnam, and students from FTU are widely recognized as being active, dynamic, and well-qualified.

FTU offers a wide range of majors in economics, business, business administration, finance and banking, law, accounting – auditing, marketing, and business foreign languages. The University currently hosts 20,000 students, of which hundreds of international students come from the USA, Europe, Australia, and Asia, who learn to lead and serve not only through a strong academic curriculum but also by participating in various extracurricular activities, from athletics to community service.

FTU scatters along the S-shape of Vietnam with 3 campuses including Hanoi – main campus, Ho Chi Minh City campus, and Quang Ninh campus. Located at the heart of Hanoi, the political and historical capital city of Vietnam, the main campus offers the conveniences of an urban center with shops, restaurants, and leisure activities to cater to any taste. Hanoi is also close to many internationally recognized sightseeing destinations including Ha Long Bay, where the third campus is nearby located. Ho Chi Minh City campus carries in itself a different taste of Vietnam as it is the center of commerce, finance, culture, and tourism in the South and Mekong Delta—the combination of neo-classical and international lifestyle. The Oriental cultural diversity and lifestyle of the people offer valuable opportunities for international students to discover.

Research Excellence & International Cooperation

FTU is committed to research, discovery, creativity, and vigorous intellectual exchange. The diversification of excellence across all disciplinary areas is considered a priority, aiming for international recognition with strong linkages with the government and business sectors.

Promoting international cooperation, especially through exchange programs, has always been recognized as an important measure for the development of FTU in an increasingly globalized environment. Towards FTU's long-term development strategy and 2030 vision, the university prioritizes establishing a global network with partner universities and organizations worldwide, providing programs taught in English for both undergraduates and graduates to attend courses and earn transferable academic credits.
